## VERIFIED FACTS

### Who can do this
Permission "Update Encounter non clinical" — Administrator, Doctor, Nurse and Facility Administrator. You also need permission to see the facility's locations.

### Before you start
The encounter exists and is still open. The facility has locations set up, and the bed or room you want belongs to the same facility as the encounter.

### Entry points
- On the encounter page, open **Encounter Actions** (shortcut `Shift+E`) and select **Assign Location**.
- Or press `l` on the encounter page.
- Or, on the **Overview** tab side panel, select **Update Location**.

### The location sheet
The sheet has two tabs: **Assign** and **History**.

**Assign a location**
1. Browse the facility's locations and select the bed or room the patient occupies. You can only assign a real place, such as a bed or a room. You cannot assign a location category.
2. Set the state of the stay. A stay can be planned, reserved or active. Only one stay can be active at a time.
3. Set the start time, and the end time when the stay has one.
4. Confirm.

**Move the patient to another location**
1. Select the new bed or room and choose the move action.
2. Care asks whether to keep the previous bed reserved for the patient, or to free it. Choose one.
3. Confirm.

**When the bed is taken**
- If an open encounter already occupies the bed, Care blocks the assignment and tells you the bed is occupied. You cannot override this. Free that bed first.
- If the encounter that occupies the bed is discharged, Care asks you to confirm before it moves the new patient in.

**History tab**
The History tab lists the patient's earlier stays for this encounter. From it you can correct the times of a stay, close a stay, or cancel a stay. Care asks you to confirm before it removes a stay.

### Rules Care applies
- The end time must be the same as or later than the start time. Otherwise Care shows "End Datetime should be greater than Start Datetime".
- A closed stay needs an end time. Otherwise Care shows "End Datetime is required for completed status".
- After you close a stay, you cannot change its state again.
- Two stays cannot overlap at the same location. Care shows "Conflict in schedule".
- The location and the encounter must belong to the same facility.
- While a patient occupies a bed, Care shows the bed as reserved. When the stay closes, Care shows the bed as available again.
- When you set the encounter status to Completed, Cancelled, Discontinued or Entered in error, Care closes any open stay for you.
- A discharged encounter still holds its location. Care only frees the bed when the stay closes or when someone marks the encounter as complete.

### Vocabulary
Care groups locations as Site, Building, Wing, Ward, Level, Corridor, Room, Bed, Vehicle, House, Cabinet, Road, Area, Jurisdiction and Virtual.

### Note
A user who belongs to the department that owns the patient's current bed can open the encounter, even when that user does not belong to the encounter's own departments.

- On the **Overview** tab side panel, select the bottom button **Mark for discharge**.

- Or open the encounter form (shortcut `Shift+U`) and select **Discharge Patient**.

### Steps
1. Start the discharge from one of the entry points above. Care sets the **Encounter Status** to **Discharged** and disables the field.
2. **Discharge Disposition** (required) — select where the patient goes. The choices are Home, Alternate home, Other health care facility, Hospice, Long term care, Left against advice, Expired, Psychiatric hospital, Rehabilitation, Skilled nursing facility and Other. ### What happens after the discharge — read this carefully
Care marks the encounter as **Discharged**, but the encounter stays open. This is deliberate:
- The care team can still change the encounter, the location, the care team, the departments and the tags.
- The patient keeps the bed until someone closes the location stay. Care only frees the bed when the stay closes or when someone marks the encounter as complete.
- To close the encounter, someone must mark it as complete. See the Complete an Encounter flow.
- Care can move a discharged encounter to another status, because Care applies no rules about which status follows which.

### Note
Your deployment can set a default discharge disposition, and it decides which encounter types your facility uses.

Title: **Initiate Patient Encounter**. Below it Care shows "Begin a new clinical encounter for" and the patient's name.

Steps and fields, in the order they appear:

### Result
Care shows "Encounter created successfully" and opens the new encounter on its Overview tab.

- "Future date is only allowed for planned encounters" — the start date is in the future but the status is not Planned. Either move the date back or set the status to Planned.
- "At least one department is required" — the Create Encounter button stays disabled until you select a department.
- "Patient already has maximum number of active encounters (5) in the facility" — the patient already has the maximum number of open encounters at this facility. Close one of them first, or continue in the existing encounter. Your deployment sets this maximum; 5 is the default.

### FHIR R5 alignment
Care's Encounter follows the FHIR R5 Encounter resource (https://build.fhir.org/encounter.html). Link it on first mention.
Care implements: status, class, priority, subject (the patient), the facility that provides the service, location, participant (as the care team), appointment, period (start and end), and the admission details (admit source, re-admission, diet preference, discharge disposition). Care records one encounter class at a time and keeps a history of every status change and class change on the encounter itself, instead of a separate FHIR EncounterHistory resource. Care stores a single free-text hospital identifier rather than the structured FHIR identifier. Care's Encounter has no diagnosis element.
Related resources an Encounter links to: Patient, Organization (the facility and its departments), Location (the bed or room), and Appointment (the booking the encounter started from).

### What an Encounter is, for the reader
An Encounter records one interaction between a patient and the facility — an outpatient visit, an admission, an observation stay, an emergency visit, a virtual consultation, or a home visit. It groups everything the care team records for that interaction. A patient can have several encounters at a facility at the same time; the deployment sets the maximum (5 unless the deployment changes it).

### Key attributes (use these user-facing labels)
- Status
- Type of Encounter (called Encounter Class on the update form)
- Priority
- Date and Time (the start), and Discharge Date and Time (the end)
- Hospital Identifier — a free-text reference number the facility uses, such as an IP, OP, OBS or EMR number
- Departments — at least one is required, and they control who can see the encounter
- Care team — the staff who look after the patient, each with a clinical role, and one marked as primary
- Current location — the bed, room or ward the patient occupies
- Tags — labels the facility configures
- Hospitalization details — Re-admission, Admit Source, Discharge Disposition, Diet Preference. These appear only for Inpatient, Observation and Emergency encounters.
- Discharge Summary Advice — free text, available once the patient is discharged

### Status values (display labels)
Planned, In Progress, On Hold, Discharged, Completed, Cancelled, Discontinued, Entered in error, Unknown.
Terminal statuses that lock the encounter: Completed, Cancelled, Discontinued, Entered in error. Once an encounter reaches one of these, nobody can edit it or record new clinical data on it.
IMPORTANT AND DELIBERATE: **Discharged is not a terminal status in Care.** A discharged encounter stays editable — the care team can still change the location, the care team, the departments and the tags. To close a discharged encounter, someone must still mark it as complete. State this plainly; it is the real behavior and the reader must know it.
Care applies no rules about which status can follow which. Any status can move to any other status while the encounter is still open.

### Type of Encounter values (display labels and meanings)
- Inpatient — Patient is admitted to the hospital
- Ambulatory — Patient visits for outpatient care
- Observation — Patient is under observation
- Emergency — Mark as emergency if immediate attention is required
- Virtual — Virtual/telehealth consultation

Note: the deployment decides which of these appear, and which one is preselected.

Stat, ASAP, Emergency, Urgent, Routine, Elective, Rush reporting, Timing critical, Callback results, Callback for scheduling, Pre-op, As needed, Use as directed. Routine is the default on the create form.

### FHIR R5 alignment
Care's Encounter follows the FHIR R5 Encounter resource (https://build.fhir.org/encounter.html). Link it on first mention.
Care implements: status, class, priority, subject (the patient), the facility that provides the service, location, participant (as the care team), appointment, period (start and end), and the admission details (admit source, re-admission, diet preference, discharge disposition). Care stores a single free-text hospital identifier rather than the structured FHIR identifier. Care's Encounter has no diagnosis element.
Related resources an Encounter links to: Patient, Organization (the facility and its departments), Location (the bed or room), and Appointment (the booking the encounter started from).

### What an Encounter is, for the reader
An Encounter records one interaction between a patient and the facility — an outpatient visit, an admission, an observation stay, an emergency visit, a virtual consultation, or a home visit. It groups everything the care team records for that interaction. A patient can have several encounters at a facility at the same time; the deployment sets the maximum (5 unless the deployment changes it).

### Key attributes (use these user-facing labels)
- Status
- Type of Encounter (called Encounter Class on the update form)
- Priority
- Date and Time (the start), and Discharge Date and Time (the end)
- Hospital Identifier — a free-text reference number the facility uses, such as an IP, OP, OBS or EMR number
- Departments — at least one is required, and they control who can see the encounter
- Care team — the staff who look after the patient, each with a clinical role, and one marked as primary
- Current location — the bed, room or ward the patient occupies
- Tags — labels the facility configures
- Hospitalization details — Re-admission, Admit Source, Discharge Disposition, Diet Preference. These appear only for Inpatient, Observation and Emergency encounters.
- Discharge Summary Advice — free text, available once the patient is discharged

Planned, In Progress, On Hold, Discharged, Completed, Cancelled, Discontinued, Entered in error, Unknown.

To close a discharged encounter, someone must still mark it as complete. State this plainly; it is the real behavior and the reader must know it.
Care applies no rules about which status can follow which. Any status can move to any other status while the encounter is still open.
